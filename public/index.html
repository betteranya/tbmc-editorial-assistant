Content clearing desk

TBMC editorial assistant

Paste a draft and the desk cleans it up. It catches the things we care about in TBMC writing and hands back a corrected version, with each change explained. Not sure a change is right? Everything is laid out so you can accept or ignore it.

What the desk checks
How it reads
  • Em dashes in moderation (flags clustering, not single uses), and no AI-sounding filler lines that add drama without saying anything new.
  • No coy setups like "Here's the thing." or "Why does this matter?" It rewrites those into real sentences.
  • Headers in sentence case, and no superlatives (best, first, only) unless we can back them up.
  • The right voice for the format: essays can use "you," but marketing body copy stays impersonal.
Naming and house style
  • Keeps our names and wording consistent, from how we write "The Better Money Company" and "clearinghouse" to the small conventions that keep copy looking like ours.
Narrative and key talking points
  • Checks copy against TBMC's narrative and approved talking points: how we describe the product, where we sit in the market, and how we talk about partners, positioning, and the things we can and can't claim yet.
  • When something runs against the narrative or needs a human eye, it flags it for review instead of quietly rewriting it.
The type of review you need
  • Everything gets a review before it goes out. The desk's job is to tell you what kind this draft needs, so you know who to loop in: a quick style pass, or a heavier sign-off for partnership news, a launch, fundraising, press, or anything legal, compliance, or regulatory.

The full narrative and talking-point rules live in the tool's settings and can be expanded over time as our messaging evolves.
